The two products separate most clearly by shopping question

Anua is the more relevant dataset when…

  • you want broader dry-skin poll representation;
  • hydration is the main captured concern you want to examine;
  • you want the higher gentle-response share as context.

Medicube is the more relevant dataset when…

  • you want broader combination-skin poll representation;
  • wrinkle / brightening has more representation in the concern poll;
  • you value the larger overall review base and more month-plus-use flags for context.

“More relevant dataset” is deliberate wording. A larger poll share shows which audience or concern is better represented in the captured data. It does not show that a product treats that concern or performs better for that skin type.

Why the identical five-star share matters

Both products show an 84% five-star share even though their displayed averages differ by 0.1. That makes the headline rating a weak tie-breaker. Rounded averages can compress the underlying distribution, and a one-decimal difference is not a controlled measure of product quality.

The broader 322,854-record K-beauty rating-distribution report shows the same compression across 34 captured product snapshots and explains why review-base size belongs beside every average.

The review-base difference is also narrower than it first appears: Medicube has 6,330 more displayed ratings, but both products already sit in a large-review context. Volume can make an aggregate more stable; it cannot verify individual buyers, remove promotional effects, or turn ratings into clinical evidence.

Hydration is the largest directional difference

Hydration accounts for 70% of Anua's captured concern poll and 47% of Medicube's, a 23-point difference. Medicube instead has more wrinkle / brightening representation, at 22% versus Anua's 3%. Soothing-redness representation is close—30% for Medicube and 28% for Anua.

These figures describe what respondents selected on two separate product pages. They do not measure moisture gain, wrinkle change, redness reduction, ingredient concentration, or a treatment outcome. Use them to decide which full product analysis contains the more relevant audience context, then check the current regional label for the features that matter to you.

What the skin-type polls can—and cannot—answer

Anua's poll is led by dry skin at 52%, while Medicube's is led by combination skin at 52%. Dry and combination respondents together account for 95% of the Anua poll and 94% of the Medicube poll. Oily-skin representation is small in both snapshots, at 4% and 6%.

Practical limit: neither dataset is strong evidence for an oily-skin-specific recommendation. The small oily-skin shares increase uncertainty; they are not negative product results.

Do the capped-sample flags pick a winner?

No. Anua has more repeat-purchase flags in its capped sample (17% versus 4%), while Medicube has more month-plus-use flags (46% versus 21%). Those flags record metadata attached to two separately selected samples of helpful review records. They do not verify why someone repurchased, whether continued use was positive, or how often all buyers returned.

The only safe reading is descriptive: the Anua sample contains more repeat-purchase labels, and the Medicube sample contains more month-plus-use labels. Treating either number as an outcome or loyalty rate would go beyond what the source data supports.

Price does not resolve the comparison

The captured Korean retail anchors were ₩27,900 for Anua on July 2 and ₩24,500 for Medicube on July 28. Both were captured as promotional packs, so the figures may cover different quantities or bundles. They are not a clean per-milliliter comparison and are not current quotes for another country.

Before buying, compare the current regional pack size, seller, ingredient label, return terms, and checkout total. A lower dated shelf figure is not enough to declare better value.

Does this prove one PDRN serum is better?

No. “PDRN” in this comparison means the term appears in the product name or captured label. The snapshots do not establish biological source, concentration, delivery, efficacy, or whether PDRN caused any reported result. The products also differ in formula, audience, promotion, price, and capture date.

If the ingredient category itself is your question, read the broader PDRN vs hyaluronic acid serum guide. If you are choosing between these two finished products, use the matched table above to identify which uncertainty you still need to resolve from current official labels.

Method and limits

This comparison reuses only fields already documented in the two dated product analyses: aggregate ratings, star shares, displayed polls, and anonymous metadata-flag counts from capped samples. No individual review text, reviewer identity, or reviewer photo is copied or translated. The editor uses code to align the fields and then checks what the numbers can and cannot support. Quick answers

Is Anua or Medicube PDRN better for dry skin?

Anua has broader dry-skin poll representation, 52% versus 42%. That makes its dataset more relevant to that audience; it does not prove better performance for dry skin.

Which has the stronger hydration review signal?

Anua's captured hydration-concern share is 70%, compared with Medicube's 47%. The figures are separate poll responses, not measured hydration outcomes.

Which looks gentler in the captured polls?

Anua has a 74% gentle-response share and a 1% separate irritation response; Medicube has 65% and 2%. Neither result predicts individual tolerance or suitability for a skin condition.
